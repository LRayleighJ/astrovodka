The HI/H₂ models

astrovodka assigns a neutral and molecular hydrogen mass to every TNG gas cell using one of four published prescriptions. This page explains what they compute, how they differ, how to choose between them, and where their assumptions bite.

Signatures, defaults and the full configuration table live in the API reference; this page is about the physics and the practical consequences.

Why not MARTINI's partition

MARTINI splits gas into HI and H₂ using a pressure-based relation calibrated on nearby galaxies. It has no redshift dependence, so at high redshift — where galaxies are denser, more metal-poor and bathed in a stronger UV field — it has no reason to be right.

The Diemer+2018 models replace it with a calculation from local conditions: gas density, metallicity, and the interstellar radiation field. That is what makes them usable across the redshift range astrovodka targets.

What the models compute

Every model produces, per gas cell, a molecular fraction

f_mol = M_H2 / (M_HI + M_H2)

and then splits the cell's neutral gas accordingly:

M_H2      = f_mol       × M_neutral
M_HI      = (1 - f_mol) × M_neutral
M_neutral = the cell's total neutral hydrogen mass

f_mol rises with gas density and metallicity — denser gas shields itself from dissociating radiation more effectively, and dust is what does the shielding, so metallicity enters through the dust-to-gas ratio. It falls with the strength of the UV field, which is what breaks H₂ apart.

Every cell also gets a neutral fraction, which accounts for ionised gas; compute_hi_mass returns m_neutral as m_hi + m_h2 by construction.

The 2×2: GD14 and K13, volume and projected

The four models are the cross product of two prescription families and two ways of estimating the gas surface density the prescriptions need.

Volume Projected
Gnedin & Draine 2014 GD14_vol GD14_map
Krumholz+ 2013 K13_vol (default) K13_map

GD14 vs K13

Both families predict f_mol from a radiation field parameter and a dust-to-gas-weighted surface density, but they were calibrated on different simulations and treat the shielding differently. In practice, on TNG galaxies, K13 tends to produce much less H₂ than GD14 — on subhalo 122 the ratio is about 0.18 (K13_vol) versus 0.43 (GD14_vol). That is a real physical disagreement between the prescriptions, not a bug, and it is the main reason for offering both.

Volume vs projected

  • The volume models estimate each cell's surface density from its own density and a Jeans-length approximation, Σ = λ_J · ρ. Purely local, no projection, no stellar particles needed.
  • The projected models build a face-on map of the galaxy and read the surface density off it. This is closer to how the observations are actually made — a telescope measures a column, not a volume density — and it is what Diemer+2018 use for the projected variants.

The projected models therefore need the stellar particles as well as the gas (to compute the stellar surface density) and cost more. They also have to make a decision about gas that falls outside the projection box — see What to watch out for.

The UV field

f_mol depends on how strong the dissociating radiation field is at each gas cell, so a model is only as good as its UV field.

The volume models take theirs from one of two sources, chosen with uv_mode:

uv_mode What it does
'adaptive_fft' (default) Convolves the galaxy's star-formation-rate distribution with a 1/r² kernel on a 3D FFT grid, giving a per-cell field that responds to where the stars actually are
'global' Uses the uniform Faucher-Giguère+2011 extragalactic background everywhere

Both apply the background as a floor, so 'global' is the limit of 'adaptive_fft' for a galaxy with no star formation. In a star-forming galaxy the two differ substantially: on the tutorial's subhalo 113, switching from the background to the adaptive field changes the HI mass by about +14%, because the strong local UV field pushes gas out of the molecular phase.

The projected models derive their UV field from the SFR map as part of the method definition, so uv_mode does not apply to them. Passing 'global' alongside a *_map model raises rather than silently ignoring the argument.

A bug worth knowing about. Before 0.3.0 the adaptive field did not work: the 3D interpolator was built with one axis' coordinates reused for all three, so nearly every particle fell outside its bounds and got fill_value=0.0. The result was a field that was numerically identical to the uniform background, meaning uv_mode='adaptive_fft' and uv_mode='global' gave the same masses and molecular masses were wrong by up to 42%. The test that guards this is placement-based rather than a pinned number — see the verification record.

Choosing a model

The default is K13_vol, and it is a reasonable default: it needs no stellar particles, is the cheapest of the four, and is the volume variant that Diemer+2018 use as their baseline.

If you want to… Use
Compare against the Diemer+2018 catalog K13_vol
Match how observations are actually made (a column through the galaxy) K13_map or GD14_map
See the effect of a different prescription switch between K13_* and GD14_*
Run with the least input data a *_vol model — no stellar particles required 
from astrovodka.sources import TNGSource

source = TNGSource(basePath=..., snapNum=99, subID=113, hi_model="K13_map")
print(source.m_HI_total, source.m_H2_g.sum(), source.f_mol_g.mean())

Because the models disagree by design, report which one you used. A single number labelled "the HI mass" is not reproducible without it.

Tuning the configuration

HIModelConfig is a frozen dataclass holding the snapshot-dependent values (h, z, box size) and the numerical settings. It can be passed as an object or as a plain dict:

from astrovodka.himodel import HIModelConfig

TNGSource(..., hi_config=HIModelConfig(h=0.6774, z=0.0, ngrid=64))
TNGSource(..., hi_config={"ngrid": 64})          # same thing, merged for you

h and z always come from the snapshot, never from the caller — passing them explicitly to TNGSource has no effect, by design, because a mismatch between the snapshot's cosmology and the model's would be silent.

The settings most likely to matter:

Field Default When to change it
ngrid 128 Lower it for speed while exploring; the UV grid becomes coarse
f_esc 0.1 If you want to test a different UV escape fraction
box_expand 1.1 How far the adaptive UV box extends beyond the gas
map_clip_outside False See below
k13_variant '2p' Which prescription sets n_CNM in the projected K13 model
vol_fine_grid 0.0 Diemer+2018 Appendix A.2's second, finer UV grid — off by default, see below

What to watch out for

Gas outside the projection box

The projected models define the galaxy as their projection box. Cells outside it get zero HI and H₂ — that is the method, and the catalog total for the method is the in-box total.

The fraction of gas mass that falls inside is reported as f_gas_in_map, and TNGSource warns when it drops below 0.9. For the tutorial's subhalo 113 it is 0.955; for subhalo 122 it is 0.883, which is low enough to be worth noticing.

If you need mass conservation instead, clamp the outside gas onto the edge pixel:

HIModelConfig(h=0.6774, z=0.0, map_clip_outside=True)

This conserves mass but piles it on the boundary, which is not obviously better — the paper does not specify a convention here, so both options are defensible and you should say which you used.

The second UV grid is off by default

Diemer+2018 Appendix A.2 runs the volume calculation twice, the second time on a grid spanning two gas half-mass radii, because a single coarse grid can underestimate the UV field at the centre of a galaxy. vol_fine_grid implements that switch and measurably moves f_mol by −3 to −5 percentage points.

It is off by default for two reasons: the paper does not say how to combine the two grids, and turning it on breaks the bit-exactness with the reference implementation that the verification record documents. Enabling it is an experiment, not a correction.

Memory

The volume models build a full ngrid³ grid and the projected models a ngrid_map² map. At the default 128 these are modest, but subhalos with more than ~10⁷ gas cells would benefit from chunking, which is not implemented.

The physics layer stands alone

astrovodka.himodel imports nothing from the observation layer, so you can use the models on a gas cell array from anywhere — another simulation, a post-processing pipeline, an interactive session — with no data cube involved:

from astrovodka.himodel import HIModelConfig, compute_hi_mass

result = compute_hi_mass(gas, model="K13_vol",
                         cfg=HIModelConfig(h=0.6774, z=0.0))
result.m_hi_msun, result.m_h2_msun
